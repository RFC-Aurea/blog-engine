## What Drives the Price of Sperm Freezing {#price-drivers}

The price of sperm freezing depends on four things: how many samples you bank, how long you store them, where you collect, and whether you need testing first. The freezing fee itself is the smallest long-term cost for most people.

### How many samples you freeze

Freezing affects sperm, so labs often ask for more than one sample. The [American Society for Reproductive Medicine](https://www.asrm.org/practice-guidance/practice-committee-documents/fertility-preservation-in-patients-with-medical-indications-a-committee-opinion-2026/) notes that, ideally, multiple samples should be obtained for cryopreservation. A [semen analysis](https://www.aureafertility.com/ai-enhanced-semen-analysis) beforehand shows your count and motility, which helps your care team decide whether one sample is enough or a second visit makes sense.

### How long you store

Storage is billed for as long as the sample is kept. Over a decade, storage costs more than six times the freezing fee, which is why the storage plan deserves as much attention as the sticker price.

### Where you collect

Collecting in the office is the lowest-cost route at $650. Our at-home option, the [Adonis Kit](https://www.aureafertility.com/adonis-kit), costs $2,250 and suits men who prefer privacy or cannot easily come to Manhattan.

### Testing before or after

Some men add a sperm DNA fragmentation test ($550), especially before IVF or after repeated losses. Our guide to [male factor infertility and IVF](https://www.aureafertility.com/post/male-factor-infertility-ivf) explains when that test adds useful information.

## Does Insurance Cover Sperm Freezing? What New York Plans Cover in 2026 {#insurance}

Insurance covers sperm freezing in New York when a medical treatment may affect your fertility. If you are freezing for planning reasons, coverage depends on your specific plan.

### When New York law requires coverage

According to the [New York State Department of Financial Services](https://www.dfs.ny.gov/consumers/health_insurance/infertility_consumer_faq_052621), New York-regulated individual, small group and large group health insurance policies must cover fertility preservation when surgery, radiation, chemotherapy or another medical treatment (including hormone therapy) may cause infertility. Covered services include collecting, freezing, preserving and storing sperm, and the law sets no specific limit on how long sperm can be stored. Your insurer may still review the request for medical necessity.

### Who the New York law does not cover

The same guidance excludes self-funded employer plans, Medicaid managed care, the Essential Plan and Medicare. Many large employers are self-funded, so call your plan and ask directly: "Is fertility preservation for iatrogenic infertility a covered benefit, and does it include storage?"

### What the national guidance says

ASRM's 2026 committee opinion calls ejaculated semen cryopreservation an established technology, names affordability as a key barrier, and states that all health insurance should cover established fertility preservation services ([ASRM, 2026](https://www.asrm.org/practice-guidance/practice-committee-documents/fertility-preservation-in-patients-with-medical-indications-a-committee-opinion-2026/)). If your plan denies a medically needed request, that opinion is useful to cite in an appeal.

## Ways to Lower the Cost Without Lowering Your Odds {#lower-cost}

You can cut the long-term sperm freezing cost by hundreds or thousands of dollars without touching sample quality. These are the levers that matter most:

1. **Pay storage yearly, not monthly.** $425 a year instead of $50 a month saves $175 a year, or $1,750 over 10 years.
2. **Test first, then decide how many samples to bank.** A $300 semen analysis can prevent paying for samples you do not need, or a second trip you did not plan for.
3. **Freeze in the office if you can.** In-office freezing costs $1,600 less than the at-home kit.
4. **Check your insurance before you pay.** If a medical treatment is behind your decision, a covered benefit may pay for freezing and storage.
5. **Review your storage once a year.** If your family is complete or the sample is no longer needed, you can stop paying storage (there is a $250 fee to discard samples).
6. **Match the sample to the treatment.** IVF with intracytoplasmic sperm injection (ICSI) needs very few sperm, so a small number of stored samples can go a long way.

If you are freezing because IVF is coming up, ask about bundling. Our [IVF for patients under 40](https://www.aureafertility.com/ivf-under-40) and [Precise IVF](https://www.aureafertility.com/affordable-ivf-nyc) programs list package prices, and our breakdown of [IVF medication costs in 2026](https://www.aureafertility.com/post/ivf-medication-costs-in-2026-a-complete-breakdown-by-protocol-drug-and-how-to-spend-less) covers the rest of the budget.

## What the Research Shows About Frozen Sperm {#research}

Frozen sperm keeps well for many years, and the research is reassuring on the two questions that drive cost: does long storage hurt quality, and does frozen sperm work as well as fresh? Freezing does lower motility once, which is why banking the right number of samples matters.

A [2025 study in Andrology](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12569725/) followed 293 men whose samples had been stored from 2.5 to more than 14 years. Storage time made no difference to motility, vitality or shape after thawing. The freeze-thaw step itself lowered progressive motility from 44.3% to 25.0%, and a man's starting motility and concentration predicted how his sample looked after storage.

On fresh versus frozen, a [2023 study of 5,335 IUI cycles in Frontiers in Reproductive Health](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10264626/) found clinical pregnancy rates of 9.4% with frozen sperm and 13.0% with fresh sperm before adjustment. After accounting for other factors, the difference was not significant, and live birth rates were similar, though frozen-sperm cycles took longer on average to reach pregnancy. With IVF, the gap matters even less: ASRM's patient fact sheet notes that very few sperm are needed to fertilize an egg in the lab, and that sperm can be stored indefinitely ([ASRM, 2023](https://www.reproductivefacts.org/news-and-publications/fact-sheets-and-infographics/cancer-and-its-impact-on-sperm/)).

| Study | Who was studied | What it found | What it means for your budget |
|---|---|---|---|
| Bier et al., Andrology, 2025 | 293 men, storage 2.5 to 14+ years | No change in motility, vitality or shape with longer storage; freezing lowered progressive motility from 44.3% to 25.0% | Long storage is safe; bank enough samples at the start |
| Cherouveim et al., Frontiers in Reproductive Health, 2023 | 5,335 IUI cycles | Clinical pregnancy 9.4% frozen vs. 13.0% fresh before adjustment; not significant after adjustment | Frozen sperm works; IUI may take an extra cycle or so |
| Lu et al., Canadian Urological Association Journal, 2026 | 24 men who banked before cancer treatment | 13% used their samples; mean cost about $2,000 over three years; 58% found it burdensome | Plan storage costs up front and review them yearly |
| ASRM patient fact sheet, 2023 | Guidance for patients | Sperm can be stored indefinitely; very few sperm needed for IVF | One well-planned bank can serve several treatments |

Paying storage monthly instead would add $175 a year, so the 10-year total would be $6,900. If you are wondering "Why is freezing sperm so expensive?", the answer is almost always the storage line, not the freezing itself.

### The treatment that uses the sample

Frozen sperm is used with intrauterine insemination (IUI) or IVF. At Aurea, an [IUI cycle](https://www.aureafertility.com/iui) is $800 (3 cycles for $2,100), and a complete IVF package is $11,200, with medications priced separately. Which one you need depends on your partner's age and fertility and on how many samples you have.

### Is it worth it?

In the Canadian cohort above, only 13% of men had used their samples within the study period ([Lu et al., 2026](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13225128/)), and half felt rushed when deciding. That is a reason to plan, not a reason to skip it: storage is insurance you hope works quietly in the background. As one forum member told another man weighing the decision, "it would be worth your time to do this." A yearly review keeps the cost tied to your real plans.

## Frequently Asked Questions {#faq}

### How much does sperm freezing cost per year?

At Aurea, in-office sperm freezing is a one-time $650, and storage is $425 a year or $50 a month. The first year therefore costs about $1,075, and each year after that costs $425 for storage. Thawing the sample later is $250.

### Does insurance cover sperm freezing in New York?

New York-regulated individual, small group and large group plans must cover sperm freezing and storage when surgery, radiation, chemotherapy or another medical treatment may cause infertility. Self-funded employer plans, Medicaid managed care, the Essential Plan and Medicare are not included in that law. Freezing for planning reasons depends on your individual plan.

### How many samples should I freeze?

ASRM recommends multiple samples when possible, because freezing lowers motility and sperm counts vary from day to day. A semen analysis first helps your care team decide whether one sample is enough. Many men bank more when the sample is meant to support several future treatments.

### How long can frozen sperm be stored?

ASRM's patient fact sheet says sperm can be stored indefinitely, and a 2025 study found no drop in motility or vitality across storage times from 2.5 to more than 14 years. The practical limit is usually cost, not quality. Reviewing your storage once a year keeps it matched to your plans.

### Can I freeze sperm at home?

Yes. Aurea's at-home option, the Adonis Kit, costs $2,250 and is designed for men who prefer to collect in private. In-office freezing costs $650 if you can come to our Manhattan office.

### Does frozen sperm work as well as fresh sperm?

For IVF with ICSI, very few sperm are needed, so frozen sperm works well. For IUI, a large 2023 study found no significant difference in clinical pregnancy after adjusting for other factors, though frozen cycles took a little longer on average. Your doctor will match the sample to the treatment that fits.
